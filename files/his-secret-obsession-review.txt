His Secret Obsession is a relationship guide by James Bauer, a coach who has worked with women on dating and commitment for around twelve years. It sells for $49 as an instant download and is built around one idea the page names "the Hero Instinct" — the claim that men have a drive to feel needed and respected that, once triggered, produces attachment.

The title is the problem. Nothing in a downloaded guide makes a man obsessed with you, and the sales page knows it: underneath the language about devotion for life, the product it describes is a set of communication behaviours. Read that way — a short book about how to ask for things in a way a man can respond to — it has something useful in it. Read the title literally and you will be disappointed within a week.

## The idea it is built on

The Hero Instinct, as the page defines it, is a man's need to feel that his efforts matter to someone — that he is the person who solves the problem, provides the thing, earns the respect. The advice that follows is behavioural: let him do things for you rather than doing them yourself, ask in a way that invites a contribution instead of a demand, show appreciation for effort rather than outcome, and give him room to step forward.

Strip the branding and this is close to standard relationship-counselling advice about bids for connection and positive reinforcement. It is not fringe. It is also not new, and the page's framing of it as a hidden drive that "not one woman in a thousand" knows about is sales copy rather than a finding.

Where the page oversells is the biology. It calls the Hero Instinct a biological drive just like hunger, thirst, and sex that becomes more powerful than all 3 combined. No measurement is offered for that. A desire to feel useful is a reasonable description of a common male experience; calling it a biological drive on the scale of hunger is a metaphor wearing a lab coat.

## What is checkable, and what is not

To the page's credit, it ends with a reference list rather than nothing. One entry is a real paper — Huo and Binning, 2008, on why the psychological experience of respect matters in group life. That is a genuine piece of social psychology about respect in groups.

It is also about groups, not about romantic attachment, and citing it does not turn the Hero Instinct into a tested theory of attraction. What the reference list shows is a coach reading widely. What it does not show is a clinical trial, a control group or a published outcome study for this program, and neither does anything else on the page.

So the fair summary: the mechanisms the book teaches are plausible and consistent with how relationship counselling generally works; the specific claim that mastering them makes a particular man devoted for life is not something anyone can guarantee, and the guarantee offered is a refund, not an outcome.

The page does promise one thing worth naming plainly. It says that within 60 days, the man of your choice will see you as his other half. That is a result claim about another person's feelings. No ebook can deliver it, and the 60-day window is a refund period, which is a different thing from a promise that the described outcome will happen.

## Who it suits, and who should not buy it

It suits a woman in an early or uncertain relationship who wants to understand how her own behaviour lands, and who is willing to change what she does rather than find a script to send. It suits readers who like a framework with a name, because the Hero Instinct gives you a lens you can apply yourself.

Do not buy it if you are in a relationship where you are frightened, being controlled, or repeatedly lied to. The advice here is about making space for a decent man to show up; it is not designed for a partner who is harming you, and applying it in that situation will read as more effort from you rather than less. That is a case for a professional, and Talkspace or a local service is the honest recommendation.

Do not buy it expecting him to change without you doing anything either. The entire premise is that you alter your half of the dynamic.
