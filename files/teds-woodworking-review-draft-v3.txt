## What the library won't do for you

Three limits matter more than any feature list.

You can't inspect it before paying. A few screenshots and a free pack of 50 plans that requires an email address is the whole preview. Everything about how 16,000 plans are actually drawn and written stays hidden until the money clears.

It isn't tuition. A plan assumes you can read a drawing, set up a saw and cut to a line. If you can't yet, a library of 16,000 drawings won't fix that. Lessons, a course or a patient neighbour will.

Size is not consistency. A collection assembled over a long period, from a team of unknown size, will contain plans of different ages and standards. The seller says each plan was built and tested before release. We have no way to check that claim across the catalogue, and you shouldn't assume uniform quality from a big number.

You have one project in mind. Search free first and read what the plan gives you. If it has a cut list, a materials list and dimensioned drawings, you are done and you have saved $67. That is the most common case and the least interesting one to be sold.

## Does the 16,000 add up?

This doesn't change whether the library is useful, but it is worth knowing before you pay for a number you can't count yourself.

The page explains where the figure came from: “a team of twelve. Full-time… Five new plans every week. Fifty weeks a year. Over two decades.” Five plans a week across fifty weeks is 250 a year, which is 5,000 over twenty years and 6,250 over twenty-five. Reaching 16,000 at that rate takes about sixty-four years.

Further down, the monthly drop is described differently: “Every month, our workshop team builds 5-8 new projects.” That is 60 to 96 a year.

Two ordinary explanations fit and the page doesn't settle which. The team may have been larger in earlier years than the twelve described today, or the library may include plans the current team didn't draft. Neither is dishonest, and the category counts do add to 16,050, which looks deliberate rather than invented.

So this is a question, not a finding. The advertised count may be accurate. What we can say is that the page's own explanation of it doesn't produce the number, and no buyer can verify 16,000 plans afterwards.
